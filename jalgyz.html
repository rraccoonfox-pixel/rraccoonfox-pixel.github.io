Jalgyz, 2026

Details

Video Mashup / Glitch

Adobe Premiere Pro | Audacity | FFmpeg | Google Colab

Resolution: 1920 × 1080 px
Duration: 2:12

About the Work

Jalgyz is a Kazakh word meaning “alone.” It is a two-minute video mashup created from two appropriated YouTube videos and two pictures connected to Kazakh culture. One video follows a man who is being pursued by horsemen, while a wolf appears as a reflection of his isolation and struggle to survive. The second video shows a large ensemble performing Kazakh music on the dombra, a traditional two-stringed instrument. I also included fragmented images of horse riders in an open landscape and the wooden structure at the top of a traditional Kazakh yurt, called a shanyraq.

I wanted to create something connected to my culture. I downloaded the two source videos through Google Colab. I also used Colab to divide the two pictures into frames and turn those frames into short videos. In Adobe Premiere Pro, I cut the original videos into short sections, rearranged them, and placed the picture sequences between changes in movement and music. My goal was to hide the original narratives and create a new sequence instead of retelling the stories of the source videos. Then I converted the completed mashup into raw data with FFmpeg and manipulated selected sections in Audacity using Bass Boost, Echo, Reverb, Phaser, and Reverse. I used these effects only on short sections so that the original imagery would remain visible. Finally, I converted the file back into a video, returned it to Premiere, and restored the original music.

The new sequence creates a contrast between individual survival and collective cultural identity. The man and the wolf are both separated from their groups, pursued, and forced to survive alone. In contrast, the dombra ensemble represents community, shared rhythm, and cultural continuity. The images of the shanyraq, horses, and landscape appear like brief pieces of cultural memory. Instead of receiving one clear story, viewers must connect the fragments and create their own interpretation. The glitch does not erase these cultural symbols; it tests how much they can change and still remain recognizable. Their survival within the broken sequence becomes a reflection of cultural endurance.
